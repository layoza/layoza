The battery lives in the frame.

No bolt-on block, no brackets. The cells sit inside the down tube, so Volta One looks like a bike, not a gadget, and the weight stays low and central where it rides best. It drops out of the underside with a key when you want to charge it indoors.

Inside the down tube

A belt, not a chain.

A toothed belt carries your pedalling to the back wheel. It runs quietly, needs no oil, and won't leave a black stripe on your trousers. Rinse it when it's muddy; check its tension at a service.

Care leaflet

The five questions we're asked most on test rides.

How far will 80 km really get me?

80 km is the upper end, on flat ground in a low assist mode. Hills, headwinds, cold weather, heavy bags and high assist all bring it down. Most riders charge once or twice a week for a daily city commute.

Does the belt need any looking after?

Very little. Don't oil it. Rinse it with water if it gets gritty, and have its tension checked when the bike is serviced.

How do I charge it?

Plug the charger into the port on the frame, or unlock the battery with the key, drop it out of the down tube and charge it indoors. It takes about three and a half hours from empty.

Can I really carry it up stairs?

At 16 kg with the battery in, most riders can lift it by the top tube and carry it on one shoulder. If you'd rather not, take the battery out first to make it lighter still.

What happens on a test ride?

About thirty minutes. We set the saddle and bars to fit you, show you the assist modes, and ride with you if you'd like. It's free, and there's nothing to sign.
