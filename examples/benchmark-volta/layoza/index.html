The battery lives inside the frame.

Nothing bolted on, nothing to steal off the rack. The cells sit inside the down tube, so Volta One looks like a bicycle and locks up like one.

When you want to charge indoors, a key releases the battery from the bottom of the tube.

A belt, where the chain used to be.

A carbon-reinforced belt runs from the crank to the rear hub motor. It needs no oil, it doesn’t rust in the rain off the Severn, and it doesn’t leave a mark on your trousers.

Questions

Anything else, ask us on a test ride. We would rather show you than tell you.

How far does 80 km really get me?

80 km is the most you’ll see on flatter routes in the lowest assist level. Hills, a heavy load, cold weather and the highest assist all bring it down. For a typical hilly Bristol commute of 10 km each way, expect several days between charges.

Can I take the battery out to charge it?

Yes. A key releases the battery from the bottom of the down tube. You can also leave it in and plug the charger into the port on the side of the frame.

Does the belt need any maintenance?

No oil and no cleaning routine. Rinse off road grit when you wash the bike, and we check the belt tension at the yearly service.

Do I need a licence or insurance to ride it?

Volta One is built to the electrically assisted pedal cycle rules in Great Britain: a 250 W motor that only helps while you pedal, up to 25 km/h. Bikes that meet those rules are ridden like ordinary bicycles, with no licence, tax or registration. Riders must be 14 or over.

Which frame size should I ride?

As a guide: S for riders about 155 to 170 cm, M for 168 to 183 cm, and L for 180 to 195 cm. If you sit between two sizes, book a test ride and we’ll bring both.
