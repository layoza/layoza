# Anonymous image-only review

Reviewed 21 October 2026. Evidence: briefs.json, the common copy in protocol.md, all eight anonymous desktop/mobile hero boards, and all sixteen anonymous full mobile screenshots. No identities, source, rationale, named renders, or key were inspected. Desktop judgments concern the initial viewport shown in the boards; lower-page judgments come from full mobile captures. Rankings combine brief fit, hierarchy, visual craft, and phone usefulness, with no speed score.

These are visual judgments from one reviewer, not a statistical result. Screenshots cannot establish interaction, keyboard access, reduced-motion behavior, actual font declarations, form validation/confirmation, data handling, or search correctness. Visible controls are assessed as affordances only. Small text in scaled full-page captures was checked against larger hero boards where possible; precise contrast compliance was not measured.

## Intervals

**Ordering: C > B ≈ D > A.** The middle tie reflects a real tradeoff: B is a more even exhibition sequence; D is a more distinctive editorial composition.

- **A:** The black gallery treatment is coherent, and the full mobile sequence gives each photograph a substantial, uncropped-looking presentation with nearby attribution. Its weakness is hierarchy: the opening statement and introduction consume most of the phone viewport before the first photograph, while the dark image surrounds make the photographs feel somewhat recessed. The phone header shows selected works and artists but no equally immediate enquiry entry. Large light enquiry block is a clear destination, though it feels abruptly appended to the black exhibition.
- **B:** Strong, quiet gallery rhythm: consistent light mounts, substantial photographs, readable artist sections, and an obvious enquiry form. The desktop staggered two-image arrangement feels deliberate. On phone the headline breaks into three large lines and postpones the first photograph; repeated generous mounts also make the exhibition long. It is polished but less photograph-first than C and less compositionally distinctive than D.
- **C:** Best interpretation of making photographs the main experience: the desktop and phone both open with two works before the exhibition statement. The black introduction becomes an effective editorial pause rather than a prerequisite. Later works, artists, and enquiry have clear changes of section and restrained typography. Weakness: two columns make the opening phone photographs and their captions substantially smaller than the later works; the first titles/credits become dense, fragile text. The blank-line style enquiry fields are also less immediately recognizable than B's bordered inputs.
- **D:** Most individual composition, using different image scales and asymmetry without a decorative color accent. The phone still gives the street photograph a commanding scale, and the fictional/local label is unusually easy to find. Weakness: Versailles is only a small opening thumbnail, while other works receive much more visual attention. The exhibition introduction arrives after two works and several separate copy blocks, so the reading path is less direct. The title, image, and enquiry link compete within the tight phone opening.

**Strongest improvement:** Keep C's photograph-first idea, but provide a larger one-column phone treatment for the first pair or a clear route to seeing them at full available width. This would improve the actual viewing experience more than adding further typography effects.

Visible brief fidelity: all four show four supplied works, artist information, monochrome presentation, and an enquiry area in the full phone captures. No visible awards or sales claims stood out. Apparent image completeness is a visual observation, not a source-level crop check.

## Willow Public Library

**Ordering: C > B > A ≈ D.** D has better typographic atmosphere than A, but its phone event collisions are a material usability penalty. A offers an earlier practical search with less refined composition.

- **A:** Strongest immediate catalogue emphasis after C; the book cards are concrete and the form has easy-to-recognize input boxes. Green/yellow blocks and the small tab-like roof shapes give it a distinct community character. Weakness: the centered hero, mixed heavy sans headings/serif body, and repeated boxed panels feel less editorially controlled than the others. The phone search placeholder is visibly truncated. All four tab-looking items compete at a narrow width, and the large top gap spends space before the main task.
- **B:** Convincing approachable editorial design, excellent compact catalogue rows, good event date/title separation, and a clear high-contrast membership ending. The desktop split hero uses the library photograph well. Weakness: on phone the large four-line heading and image push catalogue search almost to the bottom of the first viewport. The form's underline-only fields are less conspicuous than A/C/D. Its visual rhythm is sound, but the primary task could come sooner.
- **C:** Best balance of welcome and utility. The phone puts search before the photograph, while desktop keeps headline/search and imagery together. Hours appear soon after the image; catalogue entries are simple and easy to scan. The cream ground, green serif headings, yellow search block, and modest image arch work together without overpowering the content. Weakness: the phone header takes two rows, the search button wraps its arrow to another line, and some secondary labels/genre pills are small. The full phone page is long, with broad vertical spacing before and within events.
- **D:** Attractive dark catalogue panel with strong title/author grouping, recognizable search, and an inviting photographic hero. Membership controls remain clear. Weakness: the phone events have visible collisions between oversized times and event titles, particularly storytime and Make & mend. Search also starts below the first viewport, while opening hours are much later in the page. Decorative catalogue arrows imply a destination that cannot be evaluated from images. The event collision prevents the otherwise stronger visual craft from earning a higher overall place.

**Strongest improvement:** Repair D's phone event grid so time and event title have separate, stable columns or stack cleanly. For the leading C, reduce the two-row header and make the search button one clean line.

Visible brief fidelity: all four contain catalogue fixtures, hours, events, free joining, and membership forms; no software imagery or installation copy is visible. Fictional/demo disclaimers are present in the full phone pages. Search behavior, no-results feedback, and submission behavior were not tested.

## Noor Dental

**Ordering: B > D > C > A.** A has the most immediate form access, but its very large second photograph harms the overall phone experience.

- **A:** Thoughtful practical opening: an Urdu welcome, calm headline, small labelled room image, and a request form all arrive early. The form appears in the desktop hero and begins within the first phone viewport. Weakness: the consultation photograph becomes an extremely tall, narrow phone crop, occupying a disproportionate section of the page and excluding much of the interaction between the two people. It delays treatment information and makes the full page substantially longer. Early form placement also asks for details before explaining the first visit, which is a tradeoff for anxious new patients.
- **B:** Best overall pacing. A compact sage hero contains welcome, promise of explanation, clear request action, and labelled illustrative photo. The first-visit steps are distinct, the second photograph shows the consultation context, treatment descriptions are concise, and the final request form has clear boxed controls and a request-only explanation. Weakness: the round-corner hero and form are safe rather than very distinctive; small eyebrow labels and photo credits are visually secondary. The initial phone hero still takes a full viewport before the explanatory steps.
- **C:** Calm and unusually direct: the fictional/local notice is prominent early, the first-visit and treatment sections use substantial readable paragraphs, and both photo labels remain attached to their imagery. The final dark form is clearly separated. Weakness: the very large room image dominates the desktop opening and pushes explanatory material down; the phone opening also gives it considerable height. Similar serif styling for headings and body reduces hierarchy compared with B/D. The second image and full-width color changes feel more like stacked sections than a tightly composed journey.
- **D:** Strong, restrained typographic identity, clear request buttons, good numbered first-visit steps, and a sensible-sized contextual consultation image. The italic headline emphasis remains calm rather than loud. Weakness: the phone header consumes two rows and repeats the request route before a long hero. The three short reassurance statements add another pause before the substantive first-visit explanation. The final form's pale input borders are visually faint compared with B's controls, and the page has more vertical drift.

**Strongest improvement:** Replace A's enormous phone consultation crop with a bounded landscape or moderate portrait treatment that preserves the interaction. This is the largest visible defect among the Noor set. For B, modestly tighten the hero so first-visit guidance appears sooner.

Visible brief fidelity: Urdu welcome, Lahore, first-visit explanation, treatment options, preferred-date request fields, nearby illustrative-photo labels, and request-only language are visible across the four full phone pages. No invented staff identities, ratings, testimonials, or outcome guarantees stood out. Urdu direction correctness and date constraints require DOM/runtime inspection.

## Range, consistency, and limits

Anonymous letters are shuffled independently by brief. Therefore A across four briefs is not one identifiable system, and a cross-brief A/B/C/D league table would be invalid. The judgments above deliberately do not assign an overall builder winner or infer identity.

Across the whole visible set there is real task-related range: art-first/black-gallery/asymmetric exhibition approaches; several library catalogue and service-information priorities; early-form versus explanation-led dental pages; and compact versus panel-heavy operations layouts. Hearth correctly looks like an application rather than a marketing page. Intervals is mostly the most visually distinct domain.

There is also convergence: Willow and Noor repeatedly use cream grounds, green serif headings, arched photographs, thin rules, and generous spacing. These are coherent choices for the briefs, but the screenshots alone provide weaker evidence of broad creative range than the number of variants suggests. The strongest adaptation is content hierarchy, especially Intervals C and Hearth B, rather than ornamental novelty. Willow D's event collisions and Noor A's oversized phone crop are the clearest visible failures of responsive composition.

Potential reviewer disagreement: a gallery specialist could reasonably prefer D's irregular scale and editorial pace over C's immediate two-up work display; a user prioritizing immediate booking could reasonably prefer Noor A's early form over the explanation-first ordering of B/D. These alternatives do not remove the specific phone issues described above.
