The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the

In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on

## 1. The idea
A calm page where the pictures are placed like prints on a gallery wall: each at its own size and position, with text set small and light between them. Nothing lines up in the obvious way, yet everything sits on a grid you can feel. The unevenness is what makes it read as art-directed by a person: a small portrait far left, a wide landscape running off the right edge, a square that overlaps the next section. It lets photographs lead without ever becoming a slideshow.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A 12-column grid with wide margins (5–7vw). The hero is a single line of large light type (6–9vw) across the top third, and below it one photograph placed off-centre (columns 6–12, 16:10), its top edge overlapping the baseline zone of the headline by a few pixels of deliberate tension. Each following section places two or three photographs with different spans and vertical offsets: for example a 3:4 portrait in columns 2–5 starting 120px lower than a 16:10 landscape in columns 7–12; then a single small 1:1 in columns 9–11 beside a paragraph in columns 2–6. Paragraphs are short (max 40ch) and sit in the gaps. No two consecutive sections use the same arrangement. Vertical spacing is large and irregular on purpose (120–260px).

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. A light or regular weight at large size for the few headings; small, quiet text. Serif or a slender sans. Everything left-aligned to grid lines.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. A pale neutral ground with one deep ink taken from the photographs. No accent beyond a quiet link colour.

## 5. Imagery
**The project has no photographs of its own yet.** First choice: **real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections**: its city and street in old postcards and photographs, an old map of the neighbourhood, its materials up close, its trade in engravings, museum objects of its kind (references/imagery.md §3). Start with `node C:/Users/ajjma/Desktop/sharethrone/.internal/comparison/upstreams/taste/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs "conversation between"`, then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history). Download what you use into the project with `--get` and credit every one on the page (`--credits` prints the footer line). They show the world around the client, never the client's own room, dish, product, person or team. Second, for what no honest picture exists of: draw the subject's own things in code, large, in one flat graphic language and the palette's real colours (its tiles, its windows, its objects; references/imagery.md §8). Third: the dealt photographs, only where an honestly generic picture fits (the sea, the weather, a material), never one a visitor could take for the thing itself and never an unrelated scene. One treatment for all of them. If this direction is built on photographs, plan the set before building (four to six pictures of one family) and reuse them at different crops and scales instead of finding or drawing a new picture for every slot; keep drawings as `<symbol>` + `<use>` or generate them with a small script so the page stays light. List every found picture (with its source and licence), every dealt photograph and every drawing that stands in for photography in your report.

Six to nine photographs from one set, in a deliberate mix of ratios (3:4, 4:5, 1:1, 16:10, 21:9). Square corners. Captions, if any, are one short line under an image in the soft ink.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Three prints, one line
Photographs lead and the type stays small. A pale ground, a 12-column grid, 5vw side margins. The top row at 15px holds the wordmark left, three text links centred and the single action right. Three photographs with square corners hang from one shared top line at 18% of the viewport height, like prints pinned along a rail, each a different size so their bottom edges fall at three heights. A small 1:1 in columns 1-2 (about 190px). A tall 3:4 in columns 4-7, about 440px wide and 590px tall, the largest thing on screen. A 16:10 landscape in columns 9-12 that continues past the right margin and is cropped by the viewport edge. Columns 3 and 8 are left empty as breathing space. The headline sits under the small photograph, left-aligned to its left edge across columns 1-3: a light serif or slender sans at clamp(30px, 2.8vw, 44px) on two lines, followed by one sentence at 16px in the soft ink, max 30ch. One line of caption at 14px sits under the left corner of each of the other two photographs.

On a phone: The rail turns vertical, so left edges vary instead of bottoms. The tall photograph comes first at 72% of the width on the left margin; the small square follows at 38% of the width, pushed to the right margin and overlapping the first by 40px; the landscape runs full bleed below. The headline at 8vw sits beside the square on the left, with the sentence at 16px under it.
Headline: A quiet 3-6 word line at about 40px, at most 36 characters across two lines.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the brand faces.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. intro — Title, note, facts row (split)
First row: a heading of two to four words in a light weight at clamp(44px, 5vw, 76px) sits on the left margin in columns 1–6; a three-line paragraph at 16px, 260px wide, sits in columns 10–12 with its last line on the heading's baseline. Columns 7–9 stay empty. 120px lower, a second row sets three or four facts (a date, a time, a place, a price) as large light text at clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px), spread across the 12 columns with `justify-content: space-between`. Each fact has a one-line explanation at 14px directly under it in the soft ink, left-aligned to the fact. Facts are separated only by the space between them, so the row reads like the line of times on a printed programme. A photograph may start under the right half of the facts row, 5 columns wide at 4:3. Padding 140px top, 120px bottom.
On a phone: Heading at 11vw on the left margin; the note follows directly under it at 16px, full width. The facts become a two-column grid with a 32px row gap, each fact at 26px with its explanation beneath. Any photograph goes last, full width.

### 2. gallery — Photographs gathered round a heading (mosaic)
A band about one screen tall on the plain page ground, used as the page's one deliberate rest. At its visual centre sits the heading, two lines at clamp(32px, 2.6vw, 40px) in a light weight, centred, with one sentence at 17px under it and a text link beneath that. Around it six to eight small photographs are scattered irregularly across the middle columns (x 320–1120; the outer 320px on each side stay empty, equally), each 160–200px wide at about 9:10, square corners, at different heights with 60–200px of air between them. Two of them come within 40px of the heading's block, which their drift never reaches, so the cluster reads as gathered round it rather than laid on a grid. No captions and no frames. Each photograph drifts at its own rate as the band passes (4–12% of its own height on a scroll-linked timeline), so the cluster loosens and tightens; under reduced motion it stays put. The pictures come from one place or one day and share one treatment.
On a phone: Five photographs at 30% of the width sit offset left and right around the centred heading, two above it and three below, none cut by an edge. The heading drops to 7vw on two lines.

### 3. about — Pinned title, portraits pass (pinned)
A wrapper tall enough for its portraits: about 90svh per person, up to six people. A `position: sticky; top: 0` layer of 100svh holds the heading, two words at clamp(88px, 12vw, 180px), leading 0.85, centred at mid-height, in white with `mix-blend-mode: difference` so it reads over ground and photographs alike. Beneath it in the stacking order, portraits scroll past in normal flow: each a 4:5 photograph 26–30vw wide, alternately starting at column 2 and ending at column 11, 60svh apart, so each one passes behind a different half of the heading. Under each portrait's left corner: the name at 18px, the role at 14px in the soft ink, and one sentence of up to 30 words in a 280px measure. The last portrait is followed by 40svh of empty ground before the heading releases. Square corners. Nothing else is on screen.
On a phone: The heading shrinks to 17vw and pins 16px from the top instead of at mid-height. Portraits become 72% wide, alternating between the left and right margins, 48px apart, each with name, role and sentence beneath at the portrait's width.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).
- It blends text with `difference`: over anything but black, white or grey that gives random hues. Use a solid ink chosen for each ground instead.

### 4. contact — Sentence, portrait, two links (band)
One closing row of three elements hanging from the same top line, with open ground between them, on a 12-column grid. Columns 1–5: a sentence of 12–18 words at clamp(26px, 3vw, 44px), four lines, leading 1.1, saying what kind of enquiries are welcome; it is set as a paragraph and there is no separate heading. Columns 8–9: one small square portrait or photograph of the place, 150–170px, with square corners. Columns 10–12: a short question at 18px in medium weight, then 20px below it two underlined links stacked at 18px (the email address and one booking or social link), and beneath them a line at 14px in the soft ink with the city and the usual reply time. Columns 6–7 stay empty. The row has 160px of space above and 120px below. No form. On a dark ground the photograph is the only bright thing in the row.
On a phone: The sentence comes first at 7.4vw. Below it the portrait at 96px square sits at the left margin with the question and the two links to its right, top-aligned, in the remaining width. The city and reply-time line spans the full width beneath.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 5. footer — Columns above, wordmark below (stack)
The footer inverts ground and ink and fills a whole viewport (min-height 100svh), arranged as three rows in a grid (auto 1fr auto). Top row, 40px from the edge: four small columns on the 12-column grid (address, navigation, social, hours or contact), each a plain list at 15–16px with its first line in the full ink and the rest in the softer ink. Middle row: empty. Bottom row: the wordmark alone, sized to run from the left margin to the right margin on one line. Set it as inline SVG text with a `viewBox` fitted to the word and `width: 100%`, or tune `font-size` in `vw` to the letter count, so it never wraps; its baseline sits 20px above a single line of legal text at 13px. With `position: sticky; bottom: 0` and a lower `z-index` than the page, the last section slides away to uncover it.
On a phone: Columns become two by two at 15px with 32px between rows, at the top. A gap of about 20svh follows. The wordmark spans the full width at the bottom; a name longer than ten letters stacks on two lines, each fitted to the width. Legal line beneath at 12px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

## 8. Motion — Shutter image
The one hero or showcase image opens from an edge with a clip-path over about 1.1 seconds while the picture inside settles from 1.12 scale to rest. Use it once, on the image that matters most; later images simply are there. Recipe: 'Image shutter' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Placement. The differing sizes and offsets create movement on a still page.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Uneven is not random: every image edge lands on a column line, and offsets come from a small set of values. On a phone, keep the unevenness with alternating widths (100%, 72% left, 60% right) rather than stacking everything full width.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
