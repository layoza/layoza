This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
Find the printed or physical artefact that already exists in the subject's world and build the page as that thing. A wedding site is the invitation card. A restaurant is its menu. A conference is the ticket; a record is its sleeve; a shop is the receipt or the swing tag; an archive is a stack of tabbed folders; a course is the syllabus sheet. The artefact supplies everything a template would otherwise invent: its proportions, its typographic manners, its way of grouping information. Visitors recognise the object instantly, which is why these pages feel specific.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
The first screen shows the artefact as a single filled panel (paper colour on a contrasting ground) centred or offset, with believable proportions: a card at 5:7, a ticket at about 2.2:1 with a notch cut by `clip-path`, a menu as a tall sheet. All primary content lives on it, set the way that object is set: centred and symmetrical for an invitation, in tidy aligned columns for a menu or ticket. The ground behind is a flat colour or one full-bleed photograph. As the page continues, each section is another piece of the same stationery: a second card, a tear-off stub, a folded panel, always filled shapes with real edges (straight, notched, scalloped, perforated by a dotted `border` of round dots at 4px or more), never outlined boxes. The footer is the back of the object.

## 3. Type — Libre Franklin / Source Serif 4
An American gothic for headings over a sober transitional serif for reading, the pairing of a newspaper front page. Suits essays, annual reports, archives and finance pages that have real paragraphs.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Libre+Franklin:wght@700&family=Source+Serif+4:ital,wght@0,400;0,600;1,400;1,600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Libre Franklin** — weight 700 · tracking -0.035em · leading 0.92 · uppercase for the title, sentence case for section headings
- Text: **Source Serif 4** — 400 for body, 600 for emphasis, italic for quotations · leading 1.55 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
Take the manners of the artefact: a bookish serif with small caps avoided, or a blunt condensed face for tickets and posters. Sizes stay within what print would use relative to the panel; the name is large on the panel, details are small and exact.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Paper and ink plus the ground the paper sits on. Two or three flat colours in total, like a two-colour print job.

## 5. Imagery
**The project has no photographs of its own yet.** First choice: **real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections**: its city and street in old postcards and photographs, an old map of the neighbourhood, its materials up close, its trade in engravings, museum objects of its kind (references/imagery.md §3). Start with `node C:/Users/ajjma/Desktop/sharethrone/.internal/comparison/upstreams/taste/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs "little curiosity"`, then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history). Download what you use into the project with `--get` and credit every one on the page (`--credits` prints the footer line). They show the world around the client, never the client's own room, dish, product, person or team. Second, for what no honest picture exists of: draw the subject's own things in code, large, in one flat graphic language and the palette's real colours (its tiles, its windows, its objects; references/imagery.md §8). Third: the dealt photographs, only where an honestly generic picture fits (the sea, the weather, a material), never one a visitor could take for the thing itself and never an unrelated scene. One treatment for all of them. If this direction is built on photographs, plan the set before building (four to six pictures of one family) and reuse them at different crops and scales instead of finding or drawing a new picture for every slot; keep drawings as `<symbol>` + `<use>` or generate them with a small script so the page stays light. List every found picture (with its source and licence), every dealt photograph and every drawing that stands in for photography in your report.

Optional. One photograph behind the object, or small photographs placed on it like tipped-in plates. The object itself is the image.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Tabbed folder stack
A dark flat ground. A slim top row at 24px holds the wordmark dead centre and one text link far right at 15px. The headline is one centred line of heavy condensed capitals at clamp(96px, 10vw, 160px), spanning about 70% of the width, its cap line 110px from the top. Under it one supporting sentence in a serif at 20px, centred, max 46ch. From 51% of the height down the screen is a stack of five file folders: flat colour bands 86% of the viewport wide, centred, each offset 64px below the one behind so only a strip of the back four shows. Every folder has one trapezoid tab rising 40px from its top edge, cut with `clip-path: polygon()`, at its own horizontal position and in its own colour, holding one section name at 18px. The front folder is open and runs off the bottom edge; it carries the single action as a solid button at its top-left with 32px padding. Each folder links to its section and lends that section its ground colour. The tab shape returns as the button's top corners.

On a phone: The headline sits 96px down at 17vw on one line, or two at 15vw, centred, with the supporting sentence at 16px beneath. The folder stack starts at 48svh at full width: bands offset 52px, tabs 32px tall with names at 15px, staggered left, centre and right. The front folder runs off the bottom with the action button at full width inside it.
Headline: The name or a two-word statement on one line of up to about 12 characters at 10vw; two lines allowed on a phone.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Libre Franklin, text Source Serif 4) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. showcase — Output slips along the edge (full-bleed)
For subjects whose result is a piece of text or paper: a receipt, a note, a ticket, a written line. A full-bleed photograph, 100vw by 96svh, sets the scene. In the upper centre, left-aligned in a column starting at 18% of the width, two short paragraphs in paper colour at 28–32px, the first opening with the heading's two to four words; a neutral scrim sits behind that column only. Along the bottom, six paper-coloured slips stand side by side across the full width with 12px gaps, each a filled rectangle of a different height (between 26 and 44svh), their lower ends cut off by the section's bottom edge. Two or three of the slips, not all, are tilted by 1–2 degrees. Each slip carries a sample of real output in the text face at 15px: a bold first line, a divider made of typed characters, then four to eight lines.
On a phone: The photograph is 100svh. The paragraphs sit in the top third at 22px. Only three slips show, each 30vw wide, standing along the bottom at different heights, and the row scrolls sideways to reveal the rest. Slip text stays at 14px; do not scale it down.

Adjusted for this page:
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.

### 2. list — The printed menu sheet (band)
The list is one tall sheet of paper. A filled paper-colour panel 640px wide sits centred, or offset to columns 7–12, on a contrasting flat ground or a full-bleed photograph, with square corners and its top and bottom edges cut into shallow scallops by `clip-path` or an SVG `mask`. Inside, 64px padding and a centred, symmetrical setting like a printed card: the venue or section name at 40–48px, a one-line note at 15px, then three or four groups 56px apart. Each group opens with its name at 22px, centred. Items follow at 17px with 16px between them: the dish or service at the left of a 420px measure, the price at the right with tabular figures, and a one-line description at 14px in the soft ink under the name. Alignment alone links name and price; there are no leaders. The sheet ends with the opening hours as two centred lines. Holds 12–24 items.
On a phone: The sheet spans the screen with 10px of ground showing at each side and 28px padding; the scalloped edges stay at top and bottom. The measure is the full inner width and prices stay right-aligned. Group names at 20px, items at 16px.

### 3. contact — Clip-out coupon form (split)
The form is a coupon to cut out. One filled card in a strong flat colour, about 2.2:1, spans columns 2–11 with a 14px radius, with 120px of ground above and below. Semicircular notches 28px across are cut into its top and bottom edges at the 46% mark with `clip-path` or an SVG `mask`, and a vertical row of round dots (a dotted `border-left` 5px thick in the ground colour) joins the two notches as the perforation. Left of the perforation, with 48px padding: a two-line heading of three to five words at clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px) in heavy capitals at the top, and one sentence at 16px at the bottom. Right of it: four inputs stacked with 10px gaps, each a filled block 52px tall in a lighter tone of the card with its placeholder at 16px, then a full-width solid submit bar 56px tall in the darkest ink. Inputs keep visually hidden `label` elements.
On a phone: The card turns upright and spans the width with 12px margins. The perforation becomes horizontal: notches on the left and right edges, dots across. Heading and sentence sit above it; the four inputs and the submit bar sit below, each 54px tall.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names its own corners: use the one radius of this page (square unless the direction says otherwise) and one button shape.
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Libre Franklin, text Source Serif 4) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.

### 4. footer — The back of the card (stack)
The footer is the reverse side of the page's artefact, laid out as the back of a postcard. A paper-coloured panel spans columns 2–11 on the contrasting ground, about 420px tall, its top edge cut into a row of twelve semicircular scallops (an inline SVG path or `clip-path`, each scallop about 100px wide). The panel is divided by space into two halves. Left half: a short sign-off of two to four words at 32px, then two or three lines at 16px, written like a message. Right half: the address block, four left-aligned lines at 18px with 12px between them, each line a link (email, telephone, street, social); above it in the top-right corner, a stamp: one small photograph, 3:4 and 88px wide, with a 6px paper-coloured margin. Along the panel's bottom edge, a single centred line at 12px gives the copyright like a printer's imprint.
On a phone: The panel runs full width with six scallops across its top edge. The stamp sits top-right at 64px wide; the sign-off and message come first, then the address lines at 17px beneath them, then the imprint line centred at 12px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

## 8. Motion — Index with preview
Rows of text are the navigation through the work; pointing at a row brings up its image in one fixed frame and dims the other rows. On touch screens each row shows its image inline instead. Fast and exact (about 240ms crossfade), no lag behind the pointer. Recipe: 'Index with preview, anchored to the row' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Recognition. The shaped edges, the print-like alignment and the limited inks make a familiar object out of plain HTML.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Choose an artefact that truly belongs to the subject, or it becomes costume. Shape the edges with fills and `clip-path`; an outlined rectangle with a thin border is not an object. Keep text sizes readable on a phone, where the object becomes full width with its shaped edge kept at top and bottom.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
