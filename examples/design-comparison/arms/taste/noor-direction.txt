This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
Half the screen never moves. One side is a fixed photograph (or object, or product view) that stays in place for the whole visit; the other side is a calm scrolling column with everything you need to read. The fixed half keeps the feeling present while the reader gets on with the facts, like sitting in the room while reading the menu. As sections pass, the fixed image may quietly change to the one that matches.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Desktop: a two-column page, 50/50 or 45/55. One column is `position: sticky; top: 0; height: 100svh` and holds a single photograph filling it edge to edge, or the object centred on a flat field. The other column scrolls and holds all content in a single narrow measure (max 46ch) with comfortable padding (48–72px): name and one-line description first, then sections separated by generous space, then contact and footer. Headings in the scrolling column are modest (32–44px). The column grounds differ: photograph on one side, flat paper colour on the other, meeting at a clean vertical edge. On a phone the fixed half becomes a 60svh image at the top that stays sticky behind the first section, then releases.

## 3. Type — Boska
A high-contrast serif with pointed serifs and a lively italic, used roman and italic together at one size. Suits perfume, jewellery, lodges, weddings and other occasions for ceremony.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=boska@400,500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Boska** — weight 400 · tracking -0.02em · leading 0.95 · roman capitals for the main words with lowercase italic for the connecting words at the same size, or sentence case throughout; centred lines work
- Text: **Boska** — 400 for body, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.5 · tracking 0.005em
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
A text-first setting: a readable serif or a calm sans at 17–19px, with one display size for the name. Left-aligned throughout.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. The paper colour of the reading column is chosen against the photograph: a tone taken from its shadows or its lightest area. One ink, optionally one accent ink for links and the action.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. how-it-works — Itinerary on a slip (table)
The process is printed as an itinerary or receipt. One paper-coloured slip 420–480px wide stands on a contrasting flat ground at columns 7–11; its bottom edge is a zigzag cut with clip-path and its top edge is straight. The heading, two to four words at clamp(40px, 4.4vw, 68px), sits in columns 1–5 with a 30ch paragraph, top-aligned with the slip. On the slip, in the text face at 16px with tabular figures: stages grouped under time headings that carry the order ('Monday', 'Midweek', 'Friday'), each heading in bold with 28px above it. Under a heading, two to four rows; each row has what happens on the left, a duration or time on the right, and a leader of round 4px dots between. The final row is inverted, paper-coloured text on a full-width ink bar 44px tall, and states the result: what the reader holds at the end and the total time.
On a phone: The heading and paragraph come first. The slip follows at full width minus 16px margins, keeping its zigzag bottom edge. Rows keep left text, dots and right value on one line; long descriptions wrap under the left text. The inverted final row stays full width.

### 2. features — Pinned heading, passing rows (pinned)
One flat saturated field, full-bleed, as tall as its content. Columns 1–5: the heading, two or three words, at clamp(56px, 6.6vw, 104px) in the heaviest weight, position: sticky; top: 32px, so it holds while the right side travels. Columns 7–12: four to six capability blocks stacked with 120px between them. Each block is a title at 28px bold, then a two-part row: a portrait photograph at 3:4, 180–220px wide with square corners, and beside it, top-aligned, a plain list of four to six short lines at 18px with 14px between lines. Photographs alternate between the left and the right of their list, so the column does not read as a ladder. One ink colour, chosen for contrast on the field. The heading releases when the bottom of the last block reaches it.
On a phone: The heading becomes a sticky bar at the top of the section: one line at 9vw on the field colour with 12px padding, holding while blocks pass under it. Each block stacks its title, a full-width 4:3 photograph, then the list at 17px. 72px between blocks.

### 3. contact — The email as display type (statement)
The section is 100svh in the inverse of the page's colours. A single email address is set as one line of display type spanning the content width: the font-size is fitted so the address runs from margin to margin (about 7–9vw for 20 characters; break after the @ onto two lines if it is longer than 26), light or regular weight, tight tracking, sitting on a baseline 12vh above the bottom edge. It is a `mailto:` link; on hover the whole line takes the accent ink. In the top-left corner, a sentence of up to 12 words at 20–22px says what to write about. In the two bottom corners at 14px: phone and city at the left, two or three social links at the right. No heading and no form. For a pinned ending, make the section `position: sticky; bottom: 0` beneath the page so the previous section lifts off it.
On a phone: The address breaks after the @ onto two lines at 11.5vw, left-aligned, sitting at 55% of an 86svh screen. The sentence sits at the top-left at 18px. Phone, city and social links stack at the bottom-left at 15px with 12px between them.

### 4. footer — Photograph half, menu half (split)
A split footer one viewport tall. The left half is a single photograph filling columns 1–6 edge to edge, cropped hard by the left and bottom viewport edges: a close crop of the page's main subject, larger than it appeared anywhere above. The right half, on a flat field, is the site's menu set large: five or six section names stacked as links at about 2.6vw bold, 28px apart, starting at column 8 and 80px from the top. Rows are separated by space only; on hover a row's label shifts right by 12px and the others drop to the softer ink. Under the menu, 64px lower, two short blocks at 15px side by side: contact and social. The legal line runs along the very bottom of the right half at 12px. The wordmark sits small at the top of the right half, on the menu's left edge.
On a phone: The photograph leads at full width and 50svh, cropped tight. The menu follows on the flat field at 8vw per item with 20px between items, then contact and social stacked at 15px, then the legal line at 12px. The wordmark sits small above the menu.

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
A full-height photograph always in view, against a quiet, well-set column of text.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
The reading column must be genuinely well typeset, since it is half the page. Do not put cards or grids in it. Check that the fixed image crops well at short viewport heights. In blind tests the commonest complaint was monotony: several screens of the same split with only the text changing. Change what the reading half holds from section to section (a list, a large figure, a table, a full-colour panel, a drawing) and let at least one section break the split and run full width.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
