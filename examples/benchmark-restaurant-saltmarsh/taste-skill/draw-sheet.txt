This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
For a place, the photograph is the argument. One full-bleed image of where you would be standing fills the first screen, and the design steps back: the name sits along the bottom edge like a film title, or small in a corner, and nothing else interrupts the view. The page that follows keeps that restraint: few words, large pictures, long pauses. The designer's work here is editing: choosing the one frame that makes someone want to be there, and then not covering it.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: a single photograph at 100vw by 100svh, focal point kept clear of text. The name is set either very large (14–20vw) hugging the bottom edge with 16–24px of margin, or small (20–28px) top-left with a one-line description bottom-left. Navigation is two to four words in the top corners in the same small size. If text sits on the image, a soft neutral scrim under the text area only. Below, sections alternate between a short centred or left-set paragraph in a narrow column (max 52ch) with wide empty margins, and photographs shown large: one full-bleed, then a pair at different sizes, then one portrait offset to the right. Practical information (hours, address, rates, booking) is gathered once near the end in a plain two-column block.

## 3. Type — Archivo
A sturdy grotesque kept at regular weight even at 88px, so the page reads as composed rather than loud. Suits finance, infrastructure and research pages that need to look sober and current.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Archivo:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Archivo** — weight 400 · tracking -0.02em · leading 1.0 · sentence case; never bold, inactive items fade to grey
- Text: **Archivo** — 400 for body, 600 for buttons and short emphasis · leading 1.5 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
A face with some grace for the name; small, calm, well-spaced text everywhere else. Sentence case. Body 17–19px with leading around 1.55.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 6. Hero — Stepped title, two photographs
A pale neutral ground with 6vw side margins on a 12-column grid. Navigation is three small items on the top edge at 15px: one link hard left, the wordmark centred, the single action hard right. The headline is two words or short phrases on two lines in a thin serif at clamp(72px, 8.3vw, 128px), leading 1.0, stepping diagonally across the middle third of the screen: the first line starts at column 2 with its baseline at 44% of the height; the second is indented to start at column 6 and ends near column 12. Two photographs are placed against the step, both with square corners. A small 16:10 landscape in columns 10-12 hangs from 10% of the height and stops about 20px above the first line. A 4:5 photograph in columns 2-5 starts 24px under the first line's baseline, to the left of the indented second line, and is cropped by the bottom edge of the viewport. Under the second line, starting on column 6, one sentence at 20px in the soft ink, max 36ch. Columns 6-12 of the lower third stay empty.

On a phone: The step survives: the first line at 15vw flush to the 20px left margin, the second line indented by 22vw. The small landscape sits above the title at 46% of the width, pushed to the right margin. The tall photograph follows the title at 72% of the width on the left margin, cropped by the bottom of the screen; the sentence at 16px moves beneath it.
Headline: Two words or short phrases, one per line, at most 9 characters each so the indented second line fits a phone.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Archivo, text Archivo) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. about — Wide photo, colour tile, facts (mosaic)
Top: a heading of two to four words at clamp(40px, 4vw, 60px) on the left margin, then 24px below it a three-line lead paragraph at 21–22px across columns 1–6. 72px lower, two tiles sit side by side with a 16px gap. The left tile spans columns 1–8: a landscape photograph at 16:10 with one radius (0 or 10px). A short paragraph of up to 35 words at 16px is set in a solid paper-colour block 320px wide with 20px padding, placed over the photograph's top-right corner and flush with both edges. The right tile spans columns 9–12 and is a flat palette colour, 60% of the photograph's height: it holds a simple inline SVG outline of the region or the floor plan as one filled shape, with one solid dot marking the location. Under the colour tile, four label and value pairs at 14px in a two-by-two grid: altitude, distance, season, local time.
On a phone: Heading at 9vw, lead at 18px. The photograph goes full width at 4:3 and its overlaid paragraph moves beneath it as ordinary text. The colour tile follows at full width and 3:2, and the four label and value pairs sit under it in two columns.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 10px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

### 2. list — Statement, table pushed right (table)
A heading of four to six words spans the full width on two lines at clamp(40px, 4.5vw, 68px), left-aligned with its first line indented 3vw and leading 0.98. 96px below, the table is pushed into the right-hand five columns (8–12) and the left stays open. One plain word naming the table (offices, hours, rates) hangs at 14px in column 7, top-aligned with the first row. Each row is a three-part line at 15–16px with 26px between rows: the kind or day in the soft ink at the left of the measure, the place or service at 30%, and the address, time or price right-aligned, with a small arrow where the row links. Rows are separated by space only; every fourth row has 44px above it to form groups. In columns 1–4, bottom-aligned with the last row, one 4:5 photograph 22vw wide may sit alone. Holds 5–14 rows.
On a phone: Heading at 9.5vw without the indent. The table takes the full width at 16px; each row stacks the kind above the place, with the value right-aligned on the first line. The naming word is dropped because the heading already says it. The photograph follows at 60% width on the right margin.

### 3. contact — Visit card on a photograph (full-bleed)
A full-bleed photograph of the place, 100vw by 100svh, carries one solid card and nothing else. The card is a filled panel in the paper colour, fully opaque, 380px wide with 8px corners and 32px padding, placed in the upper-right corner 60px from the top and right edges (or the upper-left: pick the corner where the photograph is calmest). It holds, top to bottom: a two- or three-word title at 28px in a light weight; the address on two lines at 16px; the opening hours as three rows with days at the left and times right-aligned in tabular figures; the phone number; then, flush with the card's bottom edge and spanning its width, a solid booking button 52px tall. The card may be `position: sticky; top: 60px` inside a taller section so it holds while the photograph scrolls beneath it. The photograph's focal point must stay clear of the card. No heading outside the card.
On a phone: The photograph is 100vw by 70svh. The card leaves the corner: it spans the width with 16px margins and overlaps the photograph's bottom edge by 80px, so image and card interlock. The booking button is full width, 56px tall, at the card's bottom.

### 4. features — Rows with margin photographs (list)
Everything hangs from one vertical axis at column 5, about 35% of the width. First the heading: a statement of up to five words at clamp(40px, 5vw, 80px) starting on the axis, vertically centred in an otherwise empty 70svh of ground. Then four or five benefit rows, each starting on the same axis: a title at clamp(28px, 3vw, 48px) and under it a paragraph of 34ch at 17px. Columns 1–4 stay empty. At the right margin each row carries one photograph, top-aligned with its title and deliberately unequal: 240px wide at 4:5 in one row, 320px at 3:2 in the next, 180px square after that. Rows are separated by 96–120px of space, not lines. On a saturated field, set all type in one pale ink and leave the photographs ungraded.
On a phone: The axis collapses to the left margin. The statement sits at 9vw in 50svh of empty ground. Each row stacks its title at 26px, the paragraph, then its photograph at alternating widths: 100%, 70% flush right, 55% flush left. 64px between rows.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 5. pricing — Prices like a menu (list)
Prices set the way a good menu sets them. A heading of two to three words at 32–40px sits in columns 1–3. Columns 5–12 hold the list in two text columns with a 64px gap. Items are grouped under plain group names at 24px in medium weight with 48px above each group. Each item is a row: the name at 19px on the left, the price at the same size on the right on the same baseline, with tabular figures and the currency sign shown only once per group; beneath the name, one line of description at 16px in the softer ink, no wider than 36ch. Rows are 22px apart. Name and price are joined only by empty space (flex with `justify-content: space-between`), with no dot leaders and no lines. One small photograph (4:5, three columns wide) may sit under the heading in the left zone.
On a phone: One column. The heading at 28px, then the photograph at 60% width if used. Each item keeps name left and price right on one line at 17px, with its description beneath in the softer ink. Group names at 20px with 40px above.

### 6. testimonial — Portrait and offset quotation (split)
A 12-column row on the page ground with wide margins. A portrait of the speaker, 3:4, fills columns 2–5 and is tall, about 70svh; it takes whatever mask the page gives its photographs (square corners, or the page's one motif shape). The quotation occupies columns 7–12 and starts 120px lower than the top of the portrait: four to six lines at clamp(26px, 2.6vw, 40px) in a light or regular weight, leading 1.2, measure about 26ch. The name and role sit 32px beneath the quotation at 16px, on its left edge. A second, small photograph (1:1, columns 10–11) hangs 96px below the attribution and shows the place or the thing the speaker is talking about. There is no heading. Nothing is centred; every edge lands on a column line, and the two vertical offsets are the design.
On a phone: The portrait runs at 72% width from the left margin, 3:4. The quotation follows at 24px, full width, starting 32px beneath. Name and role sit under it. The small second photograph sits at 44% width against the right margin, 48px lower, overlapping nothing.

### 7. footer — Contact as the headline (statement)
A full-bleed field in one saturated palette colour, at least 90svh tall. Top row, 40px from the edge: two or three short notes at 15px in columns 1–7 (address, hours, a one-line description) and a back-to-top text link at the right margin. Then a tall empty gap. In the lower half, the way to get in touch is the display line: the telephone number or the email address itself, set at 8–9vw, flush left, hard against the margin, as one link. An email address may break once, before the @, giving two lines. Size it with `clamp()` so the longest line fills at least 80% of the width. Under it, one row of small text at 14px spread across three positions: links at the left, credits in the centre, city and year at the right. The wordmark appears only small, in the top row.
On a phone: Notes stack at the top at 15px. The contact line sits in the lower half at 12vw; a telephone number stays on one line, an email breaks before the @ and is sized so each half fits the width. The meta row becomes three stacked lines at 13px.

## 8. Motion — Masked headline
On load, each line of the headline rises from behind its own baseline, about 900ms with a strong ease-out, the second line 110ms after the first; the supporting sentence and the action then fade up together. Nothing else on the page performs. Because headlines are two lines at most, you author the line breaks and wrap each line yourself. Recipe: 'Masked line reveal' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 9. Where the richness comes from
The photographs, at full size, uninterrupted. Empty paper around short text makes the next image land harder.

## 10. How this direction goes wrong
A weak hero photograph cannot be fixed by typography; pick again. Do not stack a headline, a paragraph and two buttons on the image. Do not use a booking bar that covers the bottom of the view; one small action is enough.
