One cook, three boats and the marsh

Everything with fins or a shell comes off three boats working out of Wells and the staithe. We take what they land and write the bill around it.

Tamsin Rook grew up two creeks along, gutting dab on her grandfather’s boat. She cooked in Bristol and Copenhagen before coming home to light the grill in the old lifeboat shed.

Her rule is short: if it didn’t come in on the morning tide, it isn’t on the bill. Crab is dressed by hand before noon, bass goes over oak, and the samphire is picked off the flats outside the door from June to August.

When the weather keeps the boats in, the kitchen cooks from the marsh and the farms behind it. You’ll see it on the board by eleven.
