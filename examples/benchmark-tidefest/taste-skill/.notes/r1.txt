The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
The name is the headline. Set it so large that it becomes architecture: as wide as the viewport, cropped by an edge, or stacked to fill the screen. Nothing else on the first screen competes; the description of what this is drops to a single small sentence. The idea comes from treating the name's letters as material. Ask what the subject can do to its own name: a photograph of the work sits inside a counter or replaces a letter, the name repeats down the page like a reel, the letters are split to opposite corners and the page happens between them. In the study this was the single most common device among sites scoring 7.5 or more.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the wordmark spans 88–100% of the viewport width (font-size 16–24vw, set with a clamp so it never wraps), anchored either to the bottom edge with its descenders nearly touching it, or dead centre with everything else pushed to the corners. One line of supporting text at 16–20px sits in a corner on a shared margin; navigation is three or four words in the opposite corner. After the hero the page is deliberately small and calm: a narrow text column or a simple two-column grid, so the contrast with the monument holds. The name returns once more at full width as the footer, in the inverse colours. Margins are tight (20–32px) so the type reaches the edges. Two type sizes do nearly all the work: the monument and a small text size, with one mid size for section headings.

## 3. Type — Anton / Work Sans
Compressed heavy capitals stacked tight, with a neutral grotesque kept small beneath. Suits fashion drops, sport, nightlife and any page built as a poster.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Anton:wght@400&family=Work+Sans:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Anton** — weight 400 (single heavy cut) · tracking -0.01em · leading 0.86 · uppercase
- Text: **Work Sans** — 400 for body, 600 for links and short emphasis · leading 1.5 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2.5rem, 1.2rem + 5vw, 5.5rem);
}
```
One family. The display cut must survive enormous sizes: tight tracking (-0.04 to -0.07em), leading 0.8–0.9, optical kerning on. Section headings are modest (28–40px) on purpose.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 6. Hero — Inset photograph, crossing wordmark
One deep, dark flat field. A tall portrait photograph fills the left 47% of the viewport, inset 20px from the top, left and bottom edges, with a 12px radius and object-fit: cover; it is position: sticky for the length of the page while the right side scrolls. Five text links at 15px sit top-right on a 32px margin, the last of them the single action. There is no logo at the top. In the right half, at 44% of the height and centred on that half's own axis, one supporting sentence in the light ink at 24px on up to three lines, max 26ch. Along the bottom runs the name in lowercase at clamp(120px, 12vw, 200px), light or regular weight, tracking -0.04em, on one line. It starts over the photograph's lower-right corner, overlapping the image by about 10% of the viewport width (z-index above it; mix-blend-mode: difference keeps the letters legible on the picture), and ends flush with the right margin, its baseline 40px above the bottom edge.

On a phone: The photograph takes the top 58% of the screen, inset 12px, with the same radius. The name runs under it at 21vw on one line, its ascenders overlapping the photograph's bottom edge by 3vw. The sentence follows at 17px, left-aligned on a 20px margin. Links reduce to a menu word and the action on the top row, set over the photograph.
Headline: The name in lowercase on one line, up to 9 characters at this size; a longer name drops to 9vw.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names its own corners: use the one radius of this page (square unless the direction says otherwise) and one button shape.
- It blends text with `difference`: over anything but black, white or grey that gives random hues. Use a solid ink chosen for each ground instead.
- Its vw sizes assume a face of normal width: with this condensed display face, size the words to fill the measure the blueprint gives them instead of copying the vw value.

## 7. Sections, in order

### 1. list — Giant word, filled bar rows (stack)
Sized for a reading column 520–640px wide, or centred at that width on a full page. The heading is one word (menu, hours, prices) in tall condensed capitals, its `font-size` set in container units (around 46cqw for four letters, tuned per word) so the letters fill the column from edge to edge, leading 0.8. 20px below, a stack of full-width filled bars, each 56px tall with 6px gaps, in a flat tone one step from the ground, with square corners or a 6px radius. Each bar carries a category name at 16px in medium weight at the left with 18px padding and a plus sign at the right. A bar opens in place as a `details` element: it keeps its fill, and its panel lists items at 16px, name left and price right-aligned with tabular figures, 14px between items, 20px padding. Six to nine bars; the first is open by default.
On a phone: The column is the full width with 16px margins, so the one-word heading spans the screen. Bars are 54px tall with 6px gaps and open the same way. Item descriptions, if any, sit under the name at 14px in the soft ink.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names its own corners: use the one radius of this page (square unless the direction says otherwise) and one button shape.
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Anton, text Work Sans) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.

### 2. stats — Quiet figure lines (list)
Numbers stated without raising the voice. In columns 2–7, a definition-list layout: three to five rows, each a two-track grid. The first track is 132px wide and holds the figure, right-aligned, at 28–32px in medium weight and full ink with tabular numerals. The second track, after a 24px gap, holds one sentence at 16–17px in the soft ink, at most 40ch, whose first baseline matches the figure's baseline. Sentences share one left edge and figures one right edge, so a clean channel of space runs down between them. 28px between rows. The heading, two to four words at 24–28px, sits 56px above on the sentences' left edge, not the figures'. Optionally, a single small square photograph occupies columns 9–11, its top 80px lower than the first row, with no caption. Leave at least 18vh of empty ground above and below the block.
On a phone: The grid stays two-track, with the figure track narrowed to 88px and figures at 24px; sentences at 16px wrap beside them. If the photograph is used it comes after the rows at 56% width, flush right. The heading is 22px on the sentences' left edge.

### 3. pricing — The ticket (strip)
The price is printed on a ticket. On a flat contrasting ground, one filled panel about 64vw wide with 2.3:1 proportions sits centred and straight, built as a two-part grid (7fr 3fr). A half-round notch 28px across is bitten out of the top and bottom edges where the two parts meet (two absolutely positioned circles in the ground colour), and a column of round dots 6px across in the ground colour runs between the notches as the perforation. The main part holds, top-left with 40px padding, what is being bought in two to four words at about 4vw, and along its bottom three or four short lines at 17px (dates, what is included, terms) in two tidy columns. The stub holds the price at about 6vw, centred, and under it the action as a solid bar the full width of the stub, 56px tall.
On a phone: The ticket turns upright: full width less 20px margins, main part on top, stub beneath, the notches now on the left and right edges and the dot perforation running across. Title at 9vw, price at 16vw, the action bar full width inside the stub.

- Its vw sizes assume a face of normal width: with this condensed display face, size the words to fill the measure the blueprint gives them instead of copying the vw value.

### 4. how-it-works — One verb at a time (full-bleed)
Three to five full-bleed panels, each 100svh tall, stacked with no gap; each is one stage and holds one photograph covering it. In every panel a single word, the verb for that stage ('Cut', 'Fold', 'Stitch', 'Press'), is set at clamp(96px, 17vw, 300px), heavy and centred, inside a wrapper that is position: sticky; top: 50% and shifted up by half its own height. Because each word is sticky only within its own panel, it holds the centre of the screen while its photograph passes behind it, and is then pushed out by the next panel's verb. Use mix-blend-mode: difference in white, or one solid colour tested on every image. One sentence at 18px sits in the bottom-left corner of each panel over a neutral scrim limited to that corner. There is no separate heading: the verbs, read in order, are both the heading and the sequence.
On a phone: Panels stay 100svh, each photograph cropped to a vertical slice. The verb is 24vw, still sticky at the centre. The sentence sits along the bottom over a scrim at 16px, full width with 16px margins.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).
- It blends text with `difference`: over anything but black, white or grey that gives random hues. Use a solid ink chosen for each ground instead.

### 5. faq — Large questions, answers beside (table)
Every answer is open; nothing collapses. Each question-and-answer pair is a row on the 12-column grid: the question in columns 1–6 at clamp(26px, 2.6vw, 40px), medium weight, leading 1.1, no more than two lines; its answer in columns 8–12 at 17–18px with leading 1.55 and a measure near 40ch, its first baseline level with the question's first baseline. Rows are separated by 72–88px of empty space and nothing else. Five to seven rows. The section heading, two or three words, is set at the same size as the questions but in the softer ink and sits as the first row's left cell with an empty right cell, so it reads as the top of the same table rather than a title above it. Because questions are large and answers small, the left column works as a scannable index.
On a phone: Each pair stacks: question at 24px, answer 12px beneath at 17px, then 56px of space before the next. The heading leads at the same 24px in the softer ink. Answers stay open; there is no accordion on the phone either.

### 6. contact — Address in display rows (list)
The contact block is a stack of five full-width rows, every one set at the same size, clamp(44px, 6vw, 88px), regular weight, left-aligned with a 20px inset and leading 1.0: the building or venue name, the street, the city with postcode, the opening hours as one compact line, the phone number. Each row is about 150px tall with the text sitting in its lower third; rows are told apart by alternating two flat tones of the section colour as full-width bands, or by 28px of space alone. Street and phone rows are links (map, call). No heading and no form. Optionally, one small round photograph sits at the right end of the first row.

### 7. footer — Columns above, wordmark below (stack)
The footer inverts ground and ink and fills a whole viewport (min-height 100svh), arranged as three rows in a grid (auto 1fr auto). Top row, 40px from the edge: four small columns on the 12-column grid (address, navigation, social, hours or contact), each a plain list at 15–16px with its first line in the full ink and the rest in the softer ink. Middle row: empty. Bottom row: the wordmark alone, sized to run from the left margin to the right margin on one line. Set it as inline SVG text with a `viewBox` fitted to the word and `width: 100%`, or tune `font-size` in `vw` to the letter count, so it never wraps; its baseline sits 20px above a single line of legal text at 13px. With `position: sticky; bottom: 0` and a lower `z-index` than the page, the last section slides away to uncover it.
On a phone: Columns become two by two at 15px with 32px between rows, at the top. A gap of about 20svh follows. The wordmark spans the full width at the bottom; a name longer than ten letters stacks on two lines, each fitted to the width. Legal line beneath at 12px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

## 8. Motion — Scaling wordmark
The giant name responds to the scroll: as the first screen leaves, the wordmark scales down a few percent (or grows from 0.94 to 1 as the footer arrives) on a scroll-linked timeline, so the monument feels heavy. Travel stays under 8%. Static fallback where scroll timelines are unsupported. Recipe: 'Scroll-linked progress' in references/motion.md, animating `scale`.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 9. Where the richness comes from
Scale. The size of the name against the smallness of everything else is the entire visual event, so protect both ends: do not shrink the name to make room, and do not let body text creep above 20px.

## 10. How this direction goes wrong
A long name set small is just a logo. If the name has more than about 12 characters, stack it on two lines or crop it with the viewport edge; never drop below 12vw. Do not add a second big headline under the name. Do not decorate the letters.
