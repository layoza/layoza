    Two looms, two people, one shed

    Loom & Leaf is Ada and Tom (an illustrative studio), weaving in a corner of an old north-light shed outside a Lancashire mill town. The roof teeth face north so the light stays even all day: good for seeing a dropped thread.

    We weave a plain linen and a herringbone, wash every length twice so it is soft before it reaches you, then hem each piece on a treadle machine.

    1. WarpAbout 600 ends of linen are wound and beamed onto the loom.
    2. WeaveThe shuttle crosses the warp roughly 22 times for every inch of cloth.
    3. WashTwo hot washes and a line dry under the shed lights.
    4. HemTurned twice, stitched, then a woven label at the corner.
