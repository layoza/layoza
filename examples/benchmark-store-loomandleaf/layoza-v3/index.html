      One loom, two pairs of hands, a lot of weather.

      Loom & Leaf is Ruth and Tom, weaving in a converted cotton shed in Barnoldswick, where the canal meets the moor.

      We warp our dobby loom with linen yarn spun from flax, weave about 40 metres a week, and cut and hem every piece at the bench by the window. Then it goes through the wash and out on the line behind the shed, so it shrinks with us and not with you.

      Ruth weaves and dyes; Tom cuts, hems and packs. Studio people and details here are fictional, written for this demo shop.

      1. Step 1

        Flax yarn

        Undyed linen yarn arrives on cones; colours are yarn-dyed in small lots of 4 kg so stripes stay crisp after washing.

      2. Step 2

        Woven to a draft

        Each cloth has a draft. At 16 threads to the centimetre a tea towel is roughly 11,000 passes of the shuttle.

      3. Step 3

        Hemmed by hand

        Mitred corners, a double-turned hem and a hanging loop. Tom takes 12 to 20 minutes over each tea towel.

      4. Step 4

        Washed and line-dried

        Washed once at 40°C and dried on the line behind the shed, so the cloth arrives soft and already shrunk.
