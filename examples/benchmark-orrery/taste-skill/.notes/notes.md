# Orrery build notes (taste-skill run)

## Inventory (step 1)
1. Name: an orrery is a hand-cranked clockwork model of the solar system.
2. Place: Bristol harbourside, the Floating Harbour; warehouses with painted slab lettering; ferries; water at night.
3. 60 seats under one dome.
4. Shows are live and presenter-led, not films.
5. Sessions at 11:00, 14:00, 19:30.
6. Weekday school sessions.
7. Constraint: rooftop telescopes only on clear Fridays; cloud moves it into the dome.
8. Shop: star maps, telescopes, kits (planisphere for 51 degrees N).
9. Printed things: the ticket, the stub, the forecast note, a star map.
10. The real positions of the planets on a given night.
Imagery truly available: none of the project's own; brief forbids external images, so no open-collection
pictures and no dealt picsum photos. Everything drawn in SVG in one flat language.

## Argument (step 2)
1. A live planetarium on the harbour, 60 seats, presenter-led -> hero, orrery turning to tonight.
2. Four shows plus schools, with times, ages, prices -> printed show sheet (list).
3. You can book a seat now for the next 14 days -> ticket coupon form (contact) with the orrery turning to your date.
4. Fridays go up on the roof; cloud has a plan -> itinerary slip + next 3 Fridays (how-it-works).
5. The shop sells the tools to keep looking -> object on a disc, six items (showcase).
6. Members come back -> prices-like-a-menu tiers (pricing).
7. Visitors liked it -> three paper slips (testimonial; fictional samples).
8. Practical questions -> FAQ beside a drawing of the building (faq).
9. Where and when -> address in display rows + one quiet line (footer).

## Idea (step 4)
On this page, the orrery the planetarium is named after becomes the fixed half of the split stage: a
blueprint-printed orrery that turns to the real positions of the planets on the night you choose.
Competitor test: another planetarium is not named for an orrery; a generic planet picture would be.
Live thing: step the orrery a day at a time (and the booking date drives it) - planets move to their
true heliocentric longitudes for that date (mean orbital elements). Fallback without script: the static
drawing for the default date.
