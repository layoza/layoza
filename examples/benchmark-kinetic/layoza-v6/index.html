03Selected work

Selected work.

Proof looks better than promises.

Demonstration project

Halcyon Transit

Wayfinding identity2026 · Concept

A city transit identity where every route is one continuous line you can follow from map to platform sign to app.

  1. One line weight runs through map, signage and app, so the system reads as a single gesture.
  2. Route colours were tested for colour-blind riders before any were named.
  3. A modular letter "H" doubles as the station marker at every scale.
Demonstration project

Mono Atelier

E-commerce & art direction2025 · Concept

An online store that behaves like a lookbook: one garment per screen, folded into view as you scroll.

  1. Product pages load the garment first and the chrome second.
  2. A single fold transition carries you between looks.
  3. Checkout keeps the same calm grid, so nothing feels like a new site.
Demonstration project

Pulse Week

Campaign & growth2025 · Concept

A seven-day launch campaign where each day's creative grows taller than the last, on every channel at once.

  1. One bar chart becomes the campaign's whole visual language.
  2. Daily assets are generated from one template, not redrawn.
  3. Reporting uses the same bars, so results read like the ads.
Demonstration project

Orbit Labs

3D product launch2026 · Concept

A launch site for a hardware start-up where the product turns in real time as the story is told, on any phone.

  1. The 3D model is the page: each chapter changes what it shows.
  2. A still poster renders first, so the story reads before WebGL loads.
  3. Phones get a lighter model with the same silhouette.

All four projects are demonstration concepts made to show our approach, not client work.

07Insights

Notes from the studio.

Short essays on how ideas gain momentum. Open one to read the opening.

  • Colour and type tell people who you are. Motion tells them how you behave. A brand that eases in gently feels different from one that snaps into place, even when every pixel is the same. We write motion rules the way we write tone-of-voice rules: a few verbs, a few limits, and examples of what we never do.

  • A twenty-megabyte loop is the most expensive way to say almost nothing. Coded motion loads in kilobytes, responds to the visitor and can be read by a screen reader. We still shoot film, but we stopped putting it in the first second of a page.

  • Naming first locks a project into a mood before anyone knows what it needs to say. We design the behaviour, the voice and the system first, then look for the word that already fits them. Names found that way tend to survive the second year.

You won't find testimonials here. We only publish client words we can verify, and this page shows demonstration work.
