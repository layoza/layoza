One fire runs the whole day.

The fire goes in at half past four. By six the dome is white-hot, the embers are pushed to one side and the first rye tins go onto bare brick.

As the heat falls it takes the next job: buns at nine, pots of beans and stock through the quiet middle of the day. At half three we feed it again, so that when the first table sits down the hearth is back above four hundred degrees.
