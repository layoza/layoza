The oven

One fire, eighteen hours, everything we serve.

There is no gas range at Hearth & Rye. The whole day is planned around one brick oven and the way its heat rises and falls.

The first person in lights the fire a little after five. By six the dome has soaked up enough heat that the coals are raked to one side and the loaves go straight onto the oven floor, baking in a heat that falls slowly through the morning.

As it cools, the oven takes what needs gentleness: pastries, then custards, then the slow pots of beans and braises that sit in the residual warmth through the afternoon.

Around four the fire is built back up. By the time the first guests sit down, the flame is roaring at the back and the kitchen cooks from it — hot side for flatbreads and chicken, embers for vegetables, the cooling mouth for dessert. When the last plate goes out, the oven is left to hold its warmth overnight, ready to be lit again.
