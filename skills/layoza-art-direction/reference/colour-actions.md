# Colour and actions as one composition

Preserve explicit brand choices and incumbent controls. For new work, use
visual_system from the selected MCP direction: canvas, surface, ink, action_fill,
on_action, focus and control_css. These implement readable control pairs, not a
claim that the proposed palette suits the actual artwork.

## Decide where colour lives

Look at the real supplied artwork before assigning interface colours. Identify
the page ground, the area that should dominate, the action colour and the quieter
surfaces. Portfolio artwork can carry its own palette inside a frame; its green,
purple or orange does not automatically become another website accent.

Judge colour area, adjacency and repetition at actual screen size. A cobalt
ground plus navy panels plus lime controls can work as a deliberate world; it
can also compete with beige/green project art. A neutral frame, a related tint
or a smaller saturated area can bring those relationships together. Choose from
the composition, not a rule that every website must be neutral with a blue CTA.
Do not recolour client artwork to make a weak interface palette look coherent.

When colour remains uncertain, compare treatments using the same actual copy,
artwork and actions in the selected composition before spreading one across the
page. Inspect the hero and an ordinary section on desktop and phone. Explain
what gets attention first and why the image and action belong together. Do not
claim software inspected image colours unless an actual image analysis occurred.

## Give actions an authored but familiar form

Primary/secondary describes emphasis, not two equally heavy rectangles. A solid
primary with a quieter text action is a starting point; a restrained outline,
editorial action row or different arrangement may fit better. The interaction
must still look actionable without needing hover.

Distinctiveness can come from type, proportion, placement and restrained icon
detail. A strange cutout, oversized gap or novelty cursor does not make a better
CTA. Keep the label readable, the hit area honest, and focus outside clipped
ornament. Preserve normal wrapping or stack the group rather than shrinking
labels. Match icons optically to the label; do not add a second accent just to
make the button stand out.

Use checked on_action text, never assume page background works on action_fill.
Check default, hover, press, focus, disabled and loading in their real
section backgrounds. Generated CSS is a starting point; custom grounds require
checking the pairs again. aria-disabled styling alone does not disable an action.

The output should include the chosen roles and a visible composition decision,
not a harmony score, a list of prohibited colours or a promise of unique buttons.
